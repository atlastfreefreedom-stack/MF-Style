Iconography & Symbolism

Why the Horse?

M.F. Husain’s obsession with horses was neither accidental nor purely aesthetic. It was rooted deep within his childhood memories, spiritual tradition, and encounters with global art movements.

Duldul & Muharram Processions

As a boy in Indore, Husain witnessed the ceremonial wooden horses (Duldul) carried during Muharram. The fiery spirit and sculpted posture left an indelible mark on his artistic soul.

Global Synthesis: Xu Beihong & Marini

In 1952, Husain traveled to China and studied master painter Xu Beihong's sweeping ink brushstrokes. Later in Europe, Marino Marini's equestrian sculptures inspired his bold structural geometry.

Unbridled Energy & Modernity

For Husain, the horse was a symbol of post-independence India—restless, galloping forward with raw kinetic energy, defying boundaries and static traditions.

Chronology of a Vision

The Equine Timeline

Follow the evolution of Husain’s horse motif through six pivotal decades of his legendary career.

1930s – 1940s

Cinema Billboard Beginnings

Husain paints massive Bollywood movie posters in Mumbai. This early exposure to colossal canvas sizes and swift brushwork installs a monumental sense of scale that later defines his horse canvases.

1952

The Beijing Encounter with Xu Beihong

Visiting China with a cultural delegation, Husain observes Xu Beihong painting galloping horses with traditional ink wash techniques. Husain synthesizes Chinese calligraphic fluidity with Indian folk vigor.

1960s – 1970s

Peak Expressionist Series & 'Lightning'

Husain executes landmark works including the massive 60-foot mural 'Lightning' (1975) featuring twelve galloping horses. The period marks his most aggressive use of impasto, thick black contours, and raw energy.

1980s – 2000s

Mythological Epic Integration

Horses become central protagonists in his Mahabharata and Ramayana series, pulling divine chariots, symbolizing the passage of time, human struggle, and cosmic victory.
